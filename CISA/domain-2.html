CISA Domain 2: Governance and Management of IT

IT Governance ensures that IT investments support business goals, risks are managed, and resources are optimized.

  • Frameworks: COBIT, ISO/IEC 38500, ITIL
  • Principles: Strategic alignment, value delivery, risk management, performance measurement
  • Entities: Board, IT Steering Committee, CIO

Example: COBIT uses processes like EDM01 (“Ensure Governance Framework Setting and Maintenance”).

IT strategy defines long-term plans to align IT with business goals. It supports competitive advantage and digital transformation.

  • Includes mission, vision, goals, and KPIs
  • Must align with enterprise business strategy
  • Reviewed periodically to address tech trends (cloud, AI, automation)

Tools: SWOT Analysis, Balanced Scorecard

A well-defined IT organization structure supports effective governance by clarifying roles, responsibilities, and reporting.

  • Key Roles: CIO, CTO, CISO, IT Steering Committee
  • Concepts: Segregation of Duties (SoD), Lines of Defense
  • Goal: Avoid conflict of interest and increase accountability

Risk Management identifies, evaluates, and mitigates risks to IT systems and information assets.

  1. Identification: Asset mapping, threat models
  2. Assessment: Likelihood × Impact → Risk Score
  3. Mitigation: Avoid, transfer, mitigate, or accept risk

Tools: Risk Register, Risk Heat Map, ISO 27005

Information Security Governance ensures a framework exists to protect the CIA (confidentiality, integrity, availability) of information.

  • Led by CISO or Information Security Council
  • Relies on policies, controls, awareness, and monitoring
  • Frameworks: ISO 27001, NIST Cybersecurity Framework

Key Point: Security governance is strategic, not operational—it sets direction, not just configures firewalls.

Resource management ensures effective use of IT assets, staff, and budgets to meet business goals.

  • Includes: Human resources, hardware, software, IT services
  • Uses tools like inventory databases and workforce planning
  • Ensures alignment with demand forecasting and capacity planning

Performance monitoring ensures IT delivers value and meets defined service levels.

  • Metrics: Key Performance Indicators (KPIs), Service Level Agreements (SLAs)
  • Processes: Regular reporting, dashboard visualization, root cause analysis
  • Goal: Enable proactive decision-making and continuous improvement

BCM ensures the organization can maintain or quickly resume critical operations during disruption.

  • Includes BCP (Business Continuity Plan) and DRP (Disaster Recovery Plan)
  • Defines RTO (Recovery Time Objective) and RPO (Recovery Point Objective)
  • Plans must be tested regularly and updated after major changes

Example: A DR test simulates a server outage to verify system restoration within 4 hours (RTO).

Organizations must ensure IT operations adhere to legal, regulatory, and contractual requirements.

  • Examples: GDPR, HIPAA, PCI DSS, SOX
  • Activities: Internal audits, compliance checklists, legal reviews
  • Includes software license management and data privacy programs

Auditor Role: Verify documentation and evidence of compliance

Vendors, cloud providers, and contractors introduce external risks. Governance must extend to these relationships.

  • Controls: Due diligence, vendor risk assessment, contract SLAs
  • Auditors should examine access rights, encryption, and data handling by third parties
  • Regular performance reviews and audits are recommended

Flashcard

Q: What’s the difference between RTO and RPO?

A: RTO is the max time to restore a service; RPO is the max acceptable data loss (time-wise).
